# Belief Identifier prompts

Use these with the installed skill or after the assistant confirms it can read the complete single-file instructions. Replace bracketed text with your own words. The inquiry should ask one open question per turn, skip information already supplied, and acknowledge uncertainty.

## Use the conversation efficiently

1. Begin with one concrete moment: what happened, what you preferred, what you felt, and the words of your thought.
2. Answer the current question with what you actually believed, even if it differs from what you wanted to believe. “I don't know” is useful information.
3. Correct an interpretation as soon as it stops fitting. Separate what you observed from what you concluded; include practical limits that matter.
4. Check the proposed definition before trying an alternative. Notice whether the new wording feels believable and what feeling actually follows.
5. Request a connection map when several threads emerge. Use it to resume later without treating tentative ideas as established beliefs.

Use these prompts when they address the current missing connection. Let the skill choose relevant questions from its original 44-question bank and the 72 expanded prompts; coverage is useful when it deepens understanding, rather than as a quota. Several beliefs can be explored over multiple conversations. Progress means greater clarity and a reported change, not a required feeling or deadline for release.

## Follow the missing connection

> Use Belief Identifier to follow this specific reaction: [moment and response]. Recover the prediction or meaning that was active, clarify the premise connecting it to my conclusion, and explore the feared alternative when relevant. Ask one question at a time. Keep origins optional, use my wording, and check a proposed belief with me before treating it as established. If a question adds no information, investigate the next unresolved connection.

## Start from something specific

### A focused start

> Use Belief Identifier. In this specific scene, [what happened], I would have preferred [preference]. I felt [feeling]. My exact thought was, “[thought].” Start from what I have supplied and ask one open question at a time. Help me examine what that thought meant to me and what it seemed to say about me. Validate my experience while checking assumptions about other people or events.

### An uncertain preference

> I want to explore [situation], but I am unsure what I would prefer. What I know so far is [details]. Help me clarify my preference without choosing it for me. Ask one open question at a time and allow mixed feelings or uncertainty.

### A recurring thought

> The thought “[exact wording]” keeps returning when [specific circumstance]. I feel [feeling]. Help me trace its meaning in my own words. Treat any proposed self-definition as a candidate until I confirm it fits.

## Check what I actually believe

### Motivation without assumed hidden benefits

> I want to [preferred response], yet I keep [current response]. Explore what I think my current response protects or provides, then what I fear about responding as I prefer. Ask about each in turn. Do not assume I have an unconscious gain or that my reasons are already known.

### Assumptions, evidence, and real constraints

> I believe “[belief]” because [my reasons]. Help me examine the observations, assumptions, and meanings in this account. Check evidence that supports or complicates the belief, including real constraints. Ask one open question at a time; leave missing information unresolved.

### Two thoughts that conflict

> In one situation I think, “[first thought].” In another I think, “[second thought].” They seem to conflict because [reason]. Explore each thread in its own context, then examine how they fit together. Do not assume either statement captures everything I believe.

### Test a possible root

> A candidate belief is “[belief].” Check whether my actual account supports that wording. Explore contradictions and other explanations before treating it as confirmed. Distinguish supporting beliefs from any possible core belief. Do not force a deeper root if the evidence does not support one.

## Explore a credible change

### Find an alternative I can believe

> The belief we identified is “[belief].” Help me find a preferred definition that feels credible given my experience and constraints. Avoid inflated affirmations. Ask one open question at a time and check what supports the alternative in my own account.

### Letting go and an old thought returning

> I tried the preferred definition “[definition],” and the old thought “[thought]” returned during [scene]. Help me examine the meaning I gave that moment, allow the feelings I actually have, and test the preferred definition again if it still fits. Check what I report changing or staying the same. Do not promise release or treat recurrence as proof of failure.

## Consider the optional exercise later

### Ask for consent before visualization

> After we have explored the belief, explain the optional 3–5 minute love and white-light visualization and ask whether I want to try it. Wait for my consent. Before starting, check whether the emotion feels manageable. Guide it only while I want to continue, stop immediately if I ask, and afterward ask what I actually noticed. Present it as a custom combined exercise, without promising a particular result.

## Summarize, continue, or stop

### Request a table or diagram

> Summarize what we found in a compact table or diagram. Show confirmed beliefs separately from tentative candidates, and supporting beliefs separately from possible core beliefs. Include my relevant thought wording, meanings, evidence, contradictions, real constraints, preferred definition, and reported response. Mark missing information and uncertainty without filling the gaps.

### Resume from a previous summary

> Continue from this summary: [paste summary]. Use it as a record of what I previously reported. Check what still fits now, skip information already supplied, and ask one open question at a time. Keep tentative interpretations tentative unless I confirm them.

### Stop immediately

> Stop the inquiry now. Do not ask another question or continue the exercise. Leave any unfinished interpretation unresolved.
