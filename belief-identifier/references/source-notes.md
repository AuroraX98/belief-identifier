# Source coverage and attribution

Research date: October 3, 2026, user local date. Research stayed within the supplied [Essassani archive](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/). This is a broad topical review of available belief/emotion/fear/thought passages, not an exhaustive reading of the archive or every Q&A. The notes retain access limits rather than pretending every transcript was read in full.

The archive is a transcription/compilation host. Pages sometimes contain editorial additions, garbled translation, merged speaker turns, placeholder dates, or implausible reading times. Treat clearly speaker-labeled passages as the best available account of the attributed teaching, not independently authenticated recordings. Editorial overview pages help locate themes but are not direct quotations from a speaker.

## Bashar transmission passages

### B01. Changing Core Beliefs — April 30, 2006

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2006/2006-04-30/). Opened; definition/family-pattern, four-components, core-conflict, and fear passages reviewed. Describes core beliefs supporting outer layers, precise self-definition, and fear as a clue. The financial-success/good-person example reveals two definitions that make desired success incompatible with the preferred identity. Desire, definition, dedication, and detachment form its proposed method. Application: follow the user's actual identity conflict; don't assume the same one. No guarantee of instant release is retained.

### B02. Transforming Core Beliefs — May 21, 2006

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2006/2006-05-21/). Opened; personality and three-column exercise passages reviewed. Links definitions, feelings, thoughts, and behavior; compares unwanted and preferred definitions and rehearses the same scene from the alternative. If difficulty remains, asks about deeper support. Application: clarify the relevant meaning before replacement and compare actual responses. Its metaphysical and timing guarantees are not used as established outcomes.

### B03. Negative Beliefs and Letting Go — April 8, 2017

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2017/2017-04-08b/). Opened; initial core-versus-secondary discussion reviewed. A secondary belief can be identified while the underlying definition continues to sustain the pattern. Self-perpetuation is described as enabling belief-based experience. Application: examine the unresolved supporting meaning if a formulation fails to account for the response; don't announce a core release without the user's reported evidence.

### B04. Shedding New Light on Negative Beliefs

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2018/2018-11-03/). Opened; opening energy/definition material and motivational-mechanism conversation reviewed. The user supplies the energy assigned to a belief. Motivation is framed as choosing what seems to serve best; hidden definitions can make an unwanted choice seem preferable to an alternative. Application: ask about the apparent benefit and feared cost. URL date and displayed date differ; cite title/URL rather than confidently assigning a session date. Do not treat external coercion as an internal choice.

### B05. The Black Box — December 6, 2014

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2014/2014-12-06/). Opened; opening and numerous tool headings/passages reviewed. Describes negative-belief persistence through exclusivity, inversion of preferred/unpreferred, personalizing, projection, feared change, shared misery, diminished options, worst-case imagery, and diversion into secondary beliefs. Application: inspect the specific reasoning the user supplies instead of reciting or imposing the catalog. The separate neutralizers URL failed and is not claimed reviewed.

### B06. Going Gamma — November 14, 2015

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2015/2015-11-14/). Substantial speaker-labeled indexed excerpt. Encourages recognizing an unwanted belief as a belief and choosing another without invalidating oneself. It also frames increasing terror as proximity to freedom. That latter claim is not an instruction to push through panic or an outcome measure for this skill.

### B07. Being Your Natural Self — April 18, 1997

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1997/1997-04-18/). Substantial indexed fear passage. Presents fear as information about an incompatible definition and joy as a different expression of the same energy through a preferred definition. Application: examine the definition rather than fight the feeling. Automatic replacement and manifestation claims remain attributed teachings, not guaranteed effects.

### B08. A Third Reality — August 11, 2007

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2007/2007-08-11/). Substantial indexed passage. Contrasts fear with joy/love/passion according to the definitions through which energy is experienced. An unwanted reaction can reveal something previously unnoticed. Application: use a reaction to invite inquiry; don't insist the user should celebrate distress or produce joy.

### B09. Manifesting Your Dreams in the 21st Century — June 23, 2002

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2002/2002-06-23/). Substantial indexed three-components passage. Defines beliefs as what one was taught to consider true, distinguishes that from established truth, and places emotions before most thought patterns in the explanatory model. Allows thoughts to arise more directly from beliefs too. Application: use the interaction without imposing a strict consciously observed chronology.

### B10. Meeting In San Mateo — January 9, 1988

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1988/1988-01-09/). Substantial indexed emotions/definition passage. Describes emotion as activation of accepted belief, assigned meaning as relevant to reaction, and recognition of actual misconduct as distinct from condoning it. Application: separate the event from personal meaning without removing another person's responsibility.

### B11. Vision And Creativity — March 13, 1988

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1988/1988-03-13/). Opened; remembered-scene, neutralization, light-imagery, and grounding passages reviewed. Includes pale blue/golden imagery and reinterpretation of situations. The white-light specification in our exercise is user-provided. Several lines appear translated or garbled; instructions to become overwhelmed/dizzy are excluded. No factual memory erasure is established.

### B12. Resonance — September 24, 2022

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2022/2022-09-24/). Substantial speaker-labeled indexed guilt/shame passages. Guilt discussion asks about intention, learning, and doing differently; shame discussion ties perceived disconnection to worth and belonging. Application: distinguish responsibility and repair from a global identity judgment. After-death forgiveness/contact claims are spiritual material, not independently established facts.

### B13. Simultaneity and Connecting to the Oversoul — August 28, 2010

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2010/2010-08-28b/). Substantial speaker-labeled indexed excerpt about owning undeservingness, guilt, and shame. Encourages allowing the uncomfortable feeling rather than condemning it. Its claim that honest acknowledgment immediately ends the feeling is not a requirement or promise in the visualization.

### B14. Spirit Guides — August 29, 1997

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1997/1997-08-29/). Substantial indexed definition-of-doubt passage. Reframes doubt as trust directed toward an unwanted definition, shifting the question from acquiring trust to examining its object. Application: identify the particular prediction or meaning being trusted. Do not require unquestioning trust in a teacher or the assistant.

### B15. Abundance & Definition — May 22, 1988

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1988/1988-05-22c/). Substantial indexed jealousy passage. Contrasts perceived absence of a desired quality with inspiration from seeing it in another. Application: explore what the comparison means about the user's own possibility. Seeing something does not establish identical ability, resources, or outcomes.

### B16. Synchronicity — June 18, 1986

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1986/1986-06-18/). Indexed resentment/loneliness definitions and anger excerpt. Connects resentment to perceiving something as unavailable to the self and loneliness to separation. Application: investigate the meaning of the comparison or separation. Anger timing remains an attributed claim; this skill has no timer defining legitimate anger.

### B17. Made Of Ecstasy — January 30, 1988

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1988/1988-01-30/). Substantial indexed anger/doubt dialogue. Distinguishes an initial recognition of one's preference from continuing invalidation; explores what feeling right seems to provide and whether outcomes are defined as win/lose. Application: examine comparative benefit and imposed alternatives, while recognizing real harmful conduct. Garbled punctuation limits exact quotation.

### B18. Allowance — December 5, 2015

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2015/2015-12-05/). Substantial indexed self-investigation passage. Fear of finding a definition may involve fear it is true. Application: ask about that only when it fits the user's words. Do not diagnose unwillingness from an honest lack of an answer.

### B19. The Deconstruction of the Physical Mind — May 21, 2022

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2022/2022-05-21/). Substantial indexed opening/core triad passages. Describes conscious/subconscious/unconscious relationships, fear-based limits on awareness, and the personality triad. Application: consider what the user feels able to examine; no mind-reading or claim about literal brain architecture is imported.

### B20. Tis The Season — December 5, 2020

[Source](https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2020/2020-12-05/). Substantial indexed grief conversation. Presents connection beyond death and reinterpreting separation. Application: explore the user's chosen meaning of connection and loss without invalidating grieving or claiming a particular communication occurred. Continued grief does not prove defective belief.

## Research limits

Twenty Bashar transmission entries above and sixteen Elan-path transmission entries below were reviewed at the access levels recorded. This means relevant passages, not complete reading of all long sessions. Two Elan-path entries have explicit attribution ambiguity. Additional index pages and search leads were inspected but are not counted as substantive sources. Search excerpts can omit context, so avoid exact quotes from incomplete passages; revisit the exact source if context would change the answer.

The Black Box neutralizers page returned an internal fetch error on two attempts. Direct network access to the sitemap was forbidden; several archive page opens/pagination also failed. No claim is made that all material on the site was retrieved. The skill contains enough sourced mechanisms for the requested inquiry and links for relevant follow-up research. The exact combined love/white-light/forgiveness/amplification sequence is the user's custom exercise. Guaranteed relief, equal-opposite emotions, historical rewriting, and a mind unable to distinguish imagery from events were not established.

## Elan archive research

## Reviewed sources

### E01. Expressing Your Beliefs — 102

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/102/

Access: substantial indexed excerpt; direct open failed twice. Archive metadata names Elan. The opening is visibly condensed into short statements and bullets; treat its exact wording as archive presentation, potentially editorial reformulation.

Teaching: Beliefs provide a filter for both expressing and perceiving reality, and carry reinforcing logic and apparent evidence. The stated sequence is belief → emotion → thought patterns → action; action produces physical effects. For inquiry, a person's recurring action can expose an operative belief even when their stated preferred belief differs. Application: inspect what the interpretation leads the user to do, what happens next, and how they interpret that result. Do not use the self-reinforcing-evidence concept to dismiss every inconvenient observation.

### E02. Definition and Deservability — 101

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/101/

Access: long speaker-labeled indexed passages; direct opens failed. Passage attribution: Elan.

Teaching: Feelings, thoughts, and actions can reveal the definition being expressed. Elan distinguishes containing both possible beliefs from expressing one in life. Change means choosing and expressing the preferred belief rather than destroying the other one; the unwanted belief need not be invalidated. Acknowledge the current definition, feel the alternative, imagine its thinking and behavior, and act accordingly. Deservability is presented as inherent in existence, rather than earned; this is a spiritual rationale. The stated preference is within integrity, avoiding harm. Application: distinguish a rejected thought that occurs to someone from an interpretation they actually endorse; ask which is expressed in this scene.

### E03. Trust Yourself — 66

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/066/

Access: opened page, opening and relevant later passages reviewed. Attribution: Elan; later transcript has loose/partly missing turn formatting.

Teaching: Emotions, thoughts, and actions disclose beliefs. Acknowledging an unwanted belief permits choice; denying that one could believe it prevents examination. The central inquiry is: “What must I believe right now, in these circumstances, to feel this way?” Try a preferred belief, imagine its feeling and action, and compare the options. Fear is defined as expecting an unwanted reality. Anger is described as information about what does not represent one's preferred self; extended judgment is distinguished from the initial reaction. The source claims anger lasts 10–15 seconds and fully expressed emotion loosens; those are unverified universal claims, not skill guarantees. Validation is explicitly distinguished from condoning behavior.

### E04. It IS That Simple — 72

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/072/

Access: opened page, beginning through core belief-change section reviewed. Attribution: Elan; displayed date Jan 1, 0001 is a placeholder.

Teaching: A belief that change requires a difficult elaborate process is itself examinable. Decide who one prefers to be, acknowledge the current self/belief, imagine the preferred self, trust the choice, and act from it. Acknowledgment and denial are explicitly distinguished. The preferred belief is stated and felt, then its thought and action are imagined. The text presents neutral events and supplied meaning as its metaphysical model. It also claims complete creation of reality and death timing; neither belongs in the skill as established fact or as a reason to assign blame.

### E05. Believe It or Not — 71

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/071/

Access: substantial speaker-labeled indexed passages; direct open failed. Attribution: Elan; dialog is mixed in excerpts.

Teaching: Elan does not require accepting his claimed identity and offers ideas to try. Primary beliefs are distinguished from aggregate/background beliefs that reinforce them; an originating sound and its echo is the analogy. A preferred belief should be experienced emotionally through imagination so that the alternative is available to compare, rather than merely spoken as an empty statement. In the anger discussion, Elan describes shifting viewpoint rather than forcing transformation of anger itself. Application: map a core self-definition separately from its situational predictions. A merely stated affirmation is not evidence the user now believes it.

### E06. Benefit of the Doubt — 25

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/025/

Access: indexed passage reviewed; direct open failed. Speaker label not visible in excerpt; archive location is Elan.

Teaching: Fear is described as believing the unwanted outcome is most likely, which rests on underlying primary beliefs. Repetition is presented as an opportunity to examine an as-yet-unrecognized meaning; the text recommends going deeper rather than repeatedly examining the same idea. Honesty about what one believes is required before change. The claim that a symbolic situation disappears once understood is metaphysical and should not become a promise. Application: ask what makes the feared outcome appear likely and what it would mean about the user if it happened.

### E07. Circumstantial Evidence — 22

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/022/

Access: indexed passage reviewed; direct open failed. Archive location is Elan; individual speaker label not visible in excerpt.

Teaching: A primary belief is said to generate a surrounding system of background beliefs and apparently confirming circumstances. Changed circumstances can be interpreted through either the older meaning or the preferred meaning. The source presents all external evidence as an echo of belief; the skill should attribute that framing rather than use it to tell a user every observation is false. Application: identify exactly what was observed and what extra meaning the user supplied, then inspect alternative interpretations without requiring one.

### E08. Validating Yourself — 17

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/017/

Access: substantial indexed passage reviewed; direct open failed. Archive location is Elan; individual speaker label not visible in excerpt.

Teaching: A belief that joy must be earned can cause someone to invalidate excitement because they think they have not worked enough. Self-validation involves allowing what excites one within integrity. The archive presents undeservingness as a belief rather than an unavoidable property of the person. Application: investigate conditional self-worth, permission to enjoy, and whether obligation or earnedness is being added to the preferred situation. Do not presume paid work, responsibility, or commitments are only imagined restrictions.

### E09. It's Up To You — 76

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/076/

Access: opened page, opening and deservability/responsibility Q&A reviewed. Attribution: Elan.

Teaching: Society's messages about earning or not deserving a particular thing may be generalized into an identity of being undeserving. Elan links deservability to unconditional self-love and inherent existence. The text distinguishes responsibility for oneself from responding to others and advocates being an example rather than convincing others. It also emphasizes conscious versus unconsciously accepted definitions and emotion as an entry to identifying them. Application: test whether a situational disappointment has become a global self-definition. Keep the distinction between chosen meaning and practical responsibilities contextual.

### E10. You are the Transformation — 106

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/106/

Access: opened page, core opening through change-method passages reviewed. Attribution: Elan.

Teaching: Saying “I am” with a description is treated as expressing a belief. The sequence belief → feeling → thought → action → result becomes a reinforcing loop. Self-judgment about being too advanced to feel anger or fear can obstruct examination. Acknowledge the expressed belief and use imagination to experience an alternative, including how the person would act. Change is framed as emphasizing a contained alternative, not acquiring a missing belief. The teaching encourages full expression of emotion; skill application must remain voluntary and tolerable, and must not pressure someone to intensify distress to prove willingness.

### E11. Trusting Your Timing — 104

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/104/

Access: opened page; timing, relationship/completeness, and relevant indexed passages reviewed. Attribution: Elan, although some dialog turns are merged/mislabeled.

Teaching: Unavailability now is not interpreted as permanent impossibility. The imagined preferred self is used as a model for responding. In relationship inquiry, the text contrasts being incomplete without another person with being complete and choosing connection; feelings and actions differ accordingly. The source contains unverified medical claims about pain, blood clots, and healing by visualization. These must not be imported as medical advice or proof that symptoms reveal a negative belief. Application: distinguish a practical present obstacle from a global conclusion such as “I can never have this” or “I am incomplete.”

### E12. Equal Explorations — 53

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/053/

Access: substantial indexed passage reviewed; direct open failed. The passage explicitly calls the speaking energy a blend of Bashar and Elan; attribute accordingly.

Teaching: Belief supplies the foundation, emotion responds to it, and thought patterns follow emotion and belief. Negative feelings can help identify beliefs, but an unwanted belief must first be honestly acknowledged. Equal validity of possible experiences is used to restore choice. Application: treat an unwanted feeling as information to explore, not a personal failure. The general sequence is a teaching model, not grounds for assigning the same hidden belief to everyone who feels a particular emotion.

### E13. Introduction — 24

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/024/

Access: opened page and core passages reviewed. Attribution discrepancy: although hosted under Elan, the page explicitly labels its author Bashar. Call it archive-hosted Sassani teaching or note the discrepancy.

Teaching: Excitement is presented as an indicator of preferred direction; anxiety as negatively interpreted excitement. Integrity is defined through integration and no harm. A message that excitement is frivolous can interfere with following it. Emotions are presented as responses to beliefs, and recognizing an unwanted belief is the first step in changing it. Application: investigate the user's interpretation of activation and whether their preferred direction is being defined as irresponsible; never insist all anxiety is merely excitement.

### E14. Habits and Choices — 42

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/042/

Access: opened page and substantial indexed passage reviewed. Archive location is Elan; check displayed speaker when quoting directly.

Teaching: Acknowledge the unwanted belief, define and state the preferred belief, feel its implications, and act accordingly. The objection that the preferred belief is pretend while the old belief has greater power can itself expose another limiting belief. A requirement for a long complicated change process can also be examined. Application: investigate what makes the current belief feel believable and the alternative feel implausible; do not tell the user to simply believe harder or dismiss their objection.

### E15. Look Before You Leap — 80

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/080/

Access: opened page and substantive indexed passages reviewed. Attribution: Elan.

Teaching: The information is offered without requiring belief in the speaker's identity. Belief and feeling supply the foundation of thought; action expresses them. Acknowledge the unwanted interpretation, name the preferred belief, imagine someone who already holds it, feel the difference, and examine how they would act in the same circumstance. Application: use rehearsal to find one concrete preferred response. This does not establish that rehearsal guarantees an outcome or erases an actual past event.

### E16. Challenging Beliefs — 103d, Part 4 of Clear Action

URL: https://www.superphysics.org/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/elan/103d/

Access: substantial speaker-labeled indexed dialogue reviewed; direct open failed. Attribution: Elan and Asker are distinguished.

Teaching: A participant wears a silk blouse while expecting damage and describes waiting for something bad; Elan identifies the retained expectation despite the apparently new action. Application: a change of behavior alone may coexist with the old anticipated meaning. Ask what the user expected while acting and what consequence they were waiting for. The source later offers a hazardous flying-from-a-window illustration and minimizes prudence; omit those ideas from the skill. Faith-based inquiry must never become a challenge to test physical invulnerability.
