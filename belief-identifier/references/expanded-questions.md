# Expanded belief inquiry questions

These are the 72 additional questions supplied by the user, with refined wording and possible deeper follow-ups. They are proposed inquiry prompts, not Bashar or Elan quotations. The original 44 key prompts remain in [questions.md](questions.md).

Read the relevant category when choosing a more precise question. Ask only one focused question per turn. A deeper follow-up depends on the preceding answer; it is not mandatory. Check assumptions about protection, identity, origin, certainty, or restriction before using a prompt. Preserve the user's exact terms and allow them to correct every proposed belief.

The category names below match the supplied collection. They do not specify an investigation order. Current meaning and supporting premises can be investigated before optional origins; reversal and replacement prompts follow sufficient identification. A vivid answer or stronger feeling is not proof of an unconscious belief.

Ask the refined question first when it fits. Use the deeper follow-up only if the answer makes it relevant. In conversation, adapt first person to second person and replace generic words with the person's own wording.

## WHAT

Use WHAT to locate the present interpretation. Start with a specific moment. Thoughts, feelings, predictions, and beliefs are related but are not interchangeable.

### Question 01
**Original:** What do I repeatedly think, feel, or assume in this situation?

**Refined question:** In one recent moment, what thought or image kept returning?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What was I taking as true when that thought or image appeared?

### Question 02
**Original:** What do I believe this says about me, others, or life?

**Refined question:** What conclusion did I draw from this particular event?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What connects what happened to that conclusion for me?

### Question 03
**Original:** What evidence do I look for to prove this belief true?

**Refined question:** What specific observations make this belief seem true?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Which part of my conclusion, if any, goes beyond what I actually observed?

### Question 04
**Original:** What do I fear would happen if I didn’t hold this belief?

**Refined question:** If I did not rely on this belief in this situation, what would I expect to happen?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would that outcome mean to me?

### Question 05
**Original:** What am I actually protecting myself from by keeping this belief?

**Refined question:** What does responding from this belief seem to prevent, if anything?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes that consequence seem likely without my usual response?

## WHO

Use WHO when a person or relationship is already relevant. Another person's benefit, intent, or opinion is not established by imagining it. No outside source is required for a belief.

### Question 06
**Original:** Who told me, showed me, or modeled this belief to me?

**Refined question:** Whose words or behavior, if anyone's, do I associate with this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What did I conclude from what they said or did, rather than what they explicitly told me?

### Question 07
**Original:** Who benefits when I keep believing this?

**Refined question:** What changes in my relationships when I act from this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What am I assuming about how another person would respond if I acted differently?

### Question 08
**Original:** Who would I disappoint, betray, or lose if I dropped this belief?

**Refined question:** Whose reaction concerns me when I imagine responding differently, if anyone's?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would their reaction mean about my relationship with them?

### Question 09
**Original:** Who do I become if I stop identifying with this belief?

**Refined question:** How might I see myself differently if this belief were less convincing?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What feels difficult about allowing that description of myself?

### Question 10
**Original:** Who am I really when I’m not living through this belief?

**Refined question:** How do I experience myself in a moment when this belief is less active?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What remains important to me even when this belief is not directing my response?

## WHEN

Use WHEN to find context and sequence. The earliest remembered occurrence is not proof of the first occurrence. Do not assume that not changing is a deliberate excuse.

### Question 11
**Original:** When did I first start believing this?

**Refined question:** What is the earliest occasion I actually remember holding this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What meaning do I remember giving that occasion?

### Question 12
**Original:** When do I most notice this belief being triggered?

**Refined question:** At what exact point in a recent situation did this belief become convincing?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What did I notice immediately before that point?

### Question 13
**Original:** When have I unconsciously used this belief to excuse myself from change, risk, or growth?

**Refined question:** In a recent moment when I did not make a change, what reason felt convincing?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What did I expect would happen if I made the change anyway?

### Question 14
**Original:** When was the last time this belief held me back?

**Refined question:** What is the most recent decision this belief influenced?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What did I expect about the option I did not take?

### Question 15
**Original:** When do I feel the payoff or relief from keeping it?

**Refined question:** When I respond this way, what happens to the discomfort, if anything?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does that change make my response seem to accomplish?

## WHERE

Use WHERE to find a concrete setting or response. A bodily sensation can help locate the moment; its location does not identify a particular belief.

### Question 16
**Original:** Where in my past did this belief become “true” for me?

**Refined question:** What remembered setting do I associate with this belief becoming convincing?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What happened there that I took to support this conclusion?

### Question 17
**Original:** Where in my body do I feel this belief when it activates?

**Refined question:** When I notice this reaction, where do I feel a sensation in my body, if anywhere?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What thought or image accompanies that sensation, if one is available?

### Question 18
**Original:** Where in my daily life do I see this belief influencing my choices?

**Refined question:** In which specific daily choice does this belief seem most influential?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What do I predict would happen if I made a different choice there?

### Question 19
**Original:** Where would my life expand if I no longer carried this belief?

**Refined question:** In what area would a less restrictive interpretation make a meaningful difference?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What assumption currently makes that possibility seem unavailable?

## WHY

Use WHY after the actual prediction or meaning is available. Prefer what makes the belief convincing over demanding a general explanation. Habit and practical constraints can also matter.

### Question 20
**Original:** Why do I keep returning to this belief even though it limits me?

**Refined question:** What makes this belief seem convincing in the moments when I return to it?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does it explain for me that an alternative interpretation does not yet explain?

### Question 21
**Original:** Why does part of me feel safer holding on to it?

**Refined question:** What, if anything, feels safer about keeping my usual interpretation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What danger do I anticipate if I interpret the situation differently?

### Question 22
**Original:** Why does the opposite belief feel dangerous, wrong, or impossible?

**Refined question:** When I consider a specific alternative belief, what objection arises?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What am I assuming would follow if I accepted that alternative?

### Question 23
**Original:** Why did I once believe this was the only way to survive, succeed, or be accepted?

**Refined question:** What role, if any, did this belief play in an earlier situation I remember?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Which conditions from that situation do I expect to be present now?

### Question 24
**Original:** Why am I choosing to keep believing this now?

**Refined question:** What makes this belief difficult to reconsider now?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What do I expect to lose or face if it becomes less convincing?

## HOW

Use HOW to connect interpretation, response, and later meaning. Examine one example. Evidence against a belief does not automatically prove its opposite.

### Question 25
**Original:** How has this belief shaped my identity and self-image?

**Refined question:** What description of myself have I come to associate with this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What conditions do I think I must meet to be someone different?

### Question 26
**Original:** How has it influenced my relationships, success, or self-worth?

**Refined question:** What is one concrete effect of responding from this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What meaning do I give that effect when it happens?

### Question 27
**Original:** How do I reinforce this belief daily (through thoughts, language, or actions)?

**Refined question:** What do I say or do after this belief becomes active?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How do I interpret what happens afterward?

### Question 28
**Original:** How would my behavior shift if I no longer held it?

**Refined question:** In this same situation, what response might become available if the belief felt less certain?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What still makes that response difficult?

### Question 29
**Original:** How do I know, deep down, this belief is not actually true?

**Refined question:** What observations do not fit this belief as I currently state it?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How do I interpret those observations without dismissing them or forcing the opposite conclusion?

## IF / WHAT IF

Use IF / WHAT IF to explore possibilities once the present meaning is sufficiently clear. An imagined alternative is not an established belief or a promised outcome.

### Question 30
**Original:** If this belief weren’t true, what else could be true?

**Refined question:** If this conclusion were uncertain, what other explanation would fit the known facts?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does that explanation account for or leave unexplained?

### Question 31
**Original:** If I dropped this belief today, who would I become?

**Refined question:** How might I see myself if I were less certain of this belief today?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What about that possible self-description feels difficult to accept?

### Question 32
**Original:** If I fully trusted myself, how would I act instead?

**Refined question:** If I trusted myself to respond to what happens, what would I consider doing here?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What do I currently believe I could not handle?

### Question 33
**Original:** If I no longer needed this belief for protection, what would open up for me?

**Refined question:** If I could meet the protective need another way, what response would become possible?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would I need to believe about that other way for it to feel credible?

### Question 34
**Original:** What if the opposite of this belief was true right now?

**Refined question:** What is a specific alternative meaning I could consider in this situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What supports that meaning without requiring me to ignore what happened?

### Question 35
**Original:** What if keeping this belief is the only thing standing in my way?

**Refined question:** What factors besides this belief affect my options in this situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Which part of my difficulty seems connected specifically to the meaning I give it?

### Question 36
**Original:** What if I’m more powerful than the belief itself?

**Refined question:** What ability do I have to notice this belief before responding automatically?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes using that ability seem possible or difficult in this particular moment?

## SELF-DEFINITION

Use SELF-DEFINITION when the person has connected an event to who they are. Keep their own words. A belief may concern safety or a situation without involving self-worth.

### Question 37
**Original:** What do I believe this situation means about me?

**Refined question:** What do I believe this particular event says about me?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes this event seem to establish that conclusion?

### Question 38
**Original:** What identity or label have I given myself because of this belief?

**Refined question:** What exact label do I apply to myself in this situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does that label mean in concrete terms?

### Question 39
**Original:** What do I believe I am (or am not) because this belief feels true?

**Refined question:** What do I take this belief to establish about who I am or what I can do?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** In which situations do I take that conclusion to apply?

### Question 40
**Original:** What flaw, weakness, or limitation do I assume this belief proves about me?

**Refined question:** What limitation do I think this event demonstrates?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What connects this particular event to that broader limitation?

### Question 41
**Original:** What role do I cast myself into (victim, failure, outsider, unworthy, etc.) because of this belief?

**Refined question:** How do I describe my place in this situation, in my own words?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What options do I assume someone in that position does not have?

## DEFINITION OF OTHERS

Use DEFINITION OF OTHERS to distinguish observed conduct from inferred opinions, intentions, or future behavior. Actual harmful conduct can still be recognized.

### Question 42
**Original:** What do I believe this situation means about other people?

**Refined question:** What conclusion am I drawing about this particular person's behavior?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What part is something I observed, and what part is my interpretation?

### Question 43
**Original:** What story do I tell myself about how others see me because of this belief?

**Refined question:** What do I imagine this person thinks about me?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would their imagined opinion mean to me if it were accurate?

### Question 44
**Original:** Who do I make the “villain” or “judge” in this definition?

**Refined question:** What role am I assigning this person in how I understand the situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How does assigning them that role affect what I think I can do?

### Question 45
**Original:** What do I believe others will always do to me if this belief is true?

**Refined question:** What do I expect another person to do in this situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes this expectation convincing to me?

## DEFINITION OF LIFE / REALITY

Use DEFINITION OF LIFE / REALITY to notice generalization, scope, and certainty. A present obstacle is not automatically a permanent impossibility.

### Question 46
**Original:** What do I believe this situation means about the world or life itself?

**Refined question:** What broader conclusion about life do I draw from this event?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How does one event become evidence for that broader conclusion in my thinking?

### Question 47
**Original:** What do I assume life will always be because of this belief?

**Refined question:** What do I expect to keep happening because of this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Under what conditions, if any, does that expectation not fit my experience?

### Question 48
**Original:** What do I believe life is denying me or making impossible because of this belief?

**Refined question:** What possibility do I currently regard as unavailable?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes this possibility seem unavailable now?

## MEANING GIVEN TO THE SITUATION

Use MEANING GIVEN TO THE SITUATION to separate an event from the conclusion attached to it. Memory is material for inquiry, not proof of an exact origin.

### Question 49
**Original:** What meaning did I give to the event or moment that created this belief?

**Refined question:** What meaning do I remember giving the event I associate with this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Which part of that meaning was my conclusion rather than an observed fact?

### Question 50
**Original:** What did I decide about myself in that moment?

**Refined question:** What conclusion about myself do I remember making in that moment?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What made that conclusion seem necessary at the time?

### Question 51
**Original:** What “truth” did I lock in about life or people from that situation?

**Refined question:** What general expectation about people or life did I take from that situation?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What connects that particular experience to the wider expectation?

### Question 52
**Original:** How does this meaning continue to shape my choices today?

**Refined question:** In a recent choice, how did that older meaning appear in my thinking?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What did I predict would happen if I chose differently?

### Question 53
**Original:** What if the meaning I gave wasn’t actually true?

**Refined question:** How certain does this interpretation feel to me?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What other meaning would still take the actual event seriously?

## MEANING REINFORCEMENT

Use MEANING REINFORCEMENT when a repeating sequence has been described. A person can influence their response without controlling another person's conduct or every outcome.

### Question 54
**Original:** How do I keep proving this definition right?

**Refined question:** What experiences do I count as confirming this definition?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What, if anything, is new in the evidence each time?

### Question 55
**Original:** How do I filter out evidence that would disprove it?

**Refined question:** When something does not fit this belief, how do I explain it?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would I need to observe for it to count as a meaningful exception?

### Question 56
**Original:** How do I behave in ways that recreate the same meaning again and again?

**Refined question:** What tends to happen after I respond from this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** Which part of that sequence is influenced by my response, and which part is outside my control?

### Question 57
**Original:** If I stopped reinforcing this meaning, what new experiences would I create?

**Refined question:** If I responded differently, what outcome might become possible?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What would I expect that outcome to mean about the original belief?

### Question 58
**Original:** What if my old meaning was never mine to begin with?

**Refined question:** What parts of this meaning do I associate with other people's messages?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What parts do I personally accept as true now, regardless of where they came from?

## WHAT / FEAR-BASED PAYOFFS

Use WHAT / FEAR-BASED PAYOFFS when a preferred response and a repeated current response are both clear. Ask about apparent protection without requiring a hidden payoff. If the answer is 'nothing,' accept it.

### Question 59
**Original:** What hidden benefit do I believe I get from keeping this belief?

**Refined question:** What does my current response seem to provide or prevent, if anything?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What about that result makes the response seem worth repeating in the moment?

### Question 60
**Original:** What do I fear I’ll lose if I let it go?

**Refined question:** What do I expect to lose if I stop relying on this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does that possible loss mean to me?

### Question 61
**Original:** What past version of me created this belief for protection?

**Refined question:** In an earlier situation I remember, what did this belief seem to help me manage, if anything?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What do I believe would have happened then without that response?

### Question 62
**Original:** What would I have to believe instead to feel free and aligned?

**Refined question:** What interpretation would address the concern I have actually identified?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes that interpretation believable or unbelievable to me?

### Question 63
**Original:** What proof do I already have that the opposite is possible?

**Refined question:** What actual experience supports a less absolute alternative to this belief?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What does that experience establish about this belief?

### Question 64
**Original:** If I let go of this belief, what becomes available to me?

**Refined question:** What option becomes conceivable if this belief feels less certain?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What consequence of that option still concerns me?

### Question 65
**Original:** What if I no longer needed this belief to stay safe or loved?

**Refined question:** If safety or connection were possible without this response, what would feel different?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes that possibility difficult to believe now?

## WHAT ELSE / REVERSALS

Use WHAT ELSE / REVERSALS after identifying the current meaning. Test a credible alternative rather than automatically reversing the sentence. Choosing a meaning does not remove practical consequences.

### Question 66
**Original:** What else could that original situation have meant?

**Refined question:** What other interpretation could fit the original event?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How well does it account for the details I actually remember?

### Question 67
**Original:** What else could I have decided about myself back then?

**Refined question:** What other conclusion about myself could have fitted what I knew then?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What made that conclusion less available or less convincing to me?

### Question 68
**Original:** What does holding this meaning cost me today?

**Refined question:** What is one present cost of responding from this meaning?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** How does that cost compare with the consequence I fear from responding differently?

### Question 69
**Original:** What would I gain if I chose a new definition now?

**Refined question:** What might a more credible and flexible definition allow me to do or feel?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What concern would it still need to acknowledge?

### Question 70
**Original:** If the opposite meaning were true, how would I live differently right now?

**Refined question:** If I considered a workable alternative meaning, what would change in one decision today?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What do I anticipate would happen because of that change?

### Question 71
**Original:** If I already embodied the new belief, how would I speak, act, and decide today?

**Refined question:** What would one concrete response look like if this new belief felt credible?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What objection appears when I imagine making that response?

### Question 72
**Original:** What if I’ve always been free to choose a new definition in every moment?

**Refined question:** What part of my interpretation can I reconsider in this moment?

**Deeper follow-up, if relevant:** What makes reconsidering that part feel possible or difficult?
